GCSF 1.9.0 rips the songs of Resident Evil. It also rips the European release of Four Swords Adventures, the European and Japanese releases of Twilight Princess, the Japanese release of The Wind Waker, disc version 0 of Doubutsu no Mori+ and Doubutsu no Mori e+, and the other releases of Mario Party 4. The music of The Wind Waker, Twilight Princess and Pikmin 2 now starts in the state that the game gives it. A rip holds each other state of that music as a variant. Resident Evil Zero's cutscene effects now rip too. Other rips play as they did with 1.8.1.

GCSF rips Nintendo GameCube sequenced music into a PSF-family format. A rip plays with the game's code on an emulated GameCube: the game's OS, its sound library and its DSP program. The release comes with a ripper (`gcsfrip`), a renderer (`gcsfplay`), a foobar2000 component and an XMPlay plugin. `docs/gcsf.md` describes the format.

| `xmp-gcsf-1.9.0.zip` | the XMPlay plugin, for XMPlay 3.8.2 or later |

The programs run on Windows 10 or later and require no runtime DLLs. To install the component, open the file with foobar2000, or add it under Preferences › Components. To install the XMPlay plugin, unzip `xmp-gcsf-1.9.0.zip` and put `xmp-gcsf.dll` in XMPlay's folder, or in a folder inside it. Then restart XMPlay. For macOS, the README explains how to build from source.

## New in 1.9.0

- **Resident Evil.** GCSF now rips the songs of Resident Evil (USA). The first disc holds them, and the second disc holds the same files. A rip names a song after its file, for example `sg00`. Files that repeat another file, and songs that make no sound, are left out. A room's code changes the levels of some songs' channels while they play. A rip plays each song at the levels of its file. Six more rips play the mixes that rooms give two of the ambiences, for example `sg74 ch1` and `sg7a ch0-3+5`.
- **More releases.** GCSF now rips the European release of The Legend of Zelda: Four Swords Adventures, the European and Japanese releases of The Legend of Zelda: Twilight Princess, and the Japanese release of The Legend of Zelda: The Wind Waker, Zelda no Densetsu: Kaze no Takuto. Their rips play and are named as the American rips are. It also rips disc version 0 of Doubutsu no Mori+ and Doubutsu no Mori e+, and disc version 0 of Mario Party 4 (USA), its European release and its Japanese release. The Japanese release of Mario Party 4 names its songs and voices in Japanese, as its sound test does, for example `いろいろためそう`.
- **Resident Evil Zero's cutscene effects.** 18 of the game's cutscenes play their sound effects as songs. GCSF now rips them. A rip names one after its cutscene and its song ID, for example `dd01-0413`.
- **The Wind Waker's music in the game's states.** The game's sound layer starts its music by the state of the game, such as the boat's sail, Link's sword or an enemy near Link. It mutes some tracks of 14 pieces as it starts them. Some of the game's IDs start a piece in another arrangement. A rip now starts each piece as the game starts it. It also holds each other arrangement, named after the piece and the game's ID or the state, for example `kaminoto 80000125` or `sea sailing`. The battle music and three other pieces fade in over two seconds, as they do in the game. While some pieces play, the game writes a status to them, such as the phase of a boss fight. A rip holds such a piece once more for each status that changes what it plays, for example `p_ganon1 9=2`.
- **The Wind Waker's random effects.** Four of the game's effects pick the key of their note at random. They made no sound in a rip. A rip now holds each of them once for each key that makes a sound, for example `se4-0254 key 48`.
- **Twilight Princess's music in the game's states.** The game mutes some tracks of five pieces as it starts them. A rip now mutes the same tracks. Hyrule Field's day music plays one arrangement while Link walks and another while he rides. A rip holds both: `Z2BGM_FIELD_LINK_DAY` and `Z2BGM_FIELD_LINK_DAY_riding`. The game's scenes and fights give 17 pieces more states. A rip holds each other state, named after the piece and the state, for example `Z2BGM_TOAL_VILLEGE_status1`. Hyrule Field's day music also follows the field's code. A rip holds each way through the piece, in each arrangement.
- **Four Swords Adventures' second set of tracks.** The game plays `JA_BGM_SELECT` with one set of its tracks on the file select and another on the map where the players pick a stage. A rip now holds both: `JA_BGM_SELECT` and `JA_BGM_SELECT map`.
- **Pikmin 2's caves and bosses.** A cave's music changes with the floor, and a boss's music with the boss's actions. `gcsfrip` now rips a variant of the piece for each other floor's conductor, for example `caveconc_05_0`, and for each other part of a boss's music, for example `m_boss-3`. Two parts of the final boss's music have a layer that plays only while a captain stands near the boss. They get one more variant each, labelled `-near`.
- **LuigiSings while Luigi is shrunk.** A poison mushroom shrinks Luigi, and the tune then plays an octave higher. A rip now holds that state in each place, with "shrunk" at the end of its name.

## Fixed in 1.9.0

- **Pikmin 2's final boss music.** The game sets up the final boss's music with the layers of the boss's floor. A rip played the music without them, and four of its parts lost a layer. A rip now plays every part with its layers.
- **Pikmin 2's random effects.** A Wollywog's voice and two of the Giant Breadbug's sounds play only on some of the random numbers that the game draws. In a rip, the first number silenced them, and the ripper skipped them. A rip now plays each one with the first number on which it sounds.
- **Mario Kart: Double Dash!!'s level sounds.** The game starts a level sound again each frame. Six of its level sounds finish each pass, and the next start runs them again. Their rips played for ten minutes. They now loop after two passes.
- **Donkey Kong Jungle Beat's effects.** Some of the game's effects add a value that the game gives them to the key of a note. A rip now starts such an effect with the value that the game gives it first. The pause menu's clap, for one, now plays a note. The effect that plays a combo's tones now plays over a stage's music. It made no sound before. Ten chord effects whose tracks all start at once made no sound in a rip. They now play.
- **Fade-ins of MusyX songs.** Star Fox Adventures raises every song to its volume over half a second. Resident Evil Zero's cutscenes raise their songs over 133 ms. A rip started each song at its volume at once. It now fades in as the game does.

## Other changes

- GCSF's emulation of the DSP and the CPU runs faster. Rips play more easily in real time on slower computers.
- At the start and after a seek, the foobar2000 component and the XMPlay plugin now wait until three seconds are rendered ahead before they play.

## Known limitations

- Only games with a driver profile can be ripped.
- Sounds that play until the game stops them, such as ambiences, get two loops and a fade when the ripper finds their loops. A sound that settles on a held note loops there. Other sounds play until their notes end, for at most ten minutes.
- Only sequenced sound is ripped. Use tools such as vgmstream for streams (`.ast`, `.adp`, `.str`). Navi Trackers, a mode that only Four Swords Plus has, plays its music from streams. Star Wars Rogue Squadron III: Rebel Strike streams all of its music.
- The ripper leaves out the effects of one of Tales of Symphonia's voice files. That file holds no pool of samples. MusyX can't load its group.
- GCSF doesn't rip Resident Evil's sound effects. A room's code changes the levels of some songs' channels while they play, above all those of the ambiences. A rip plays each song at the levels of its file, apart from the six mixes.
- A rip plays each of the effects of Metroid Prime, Metroid Prime 2: Echoes and Paper Mario: The Thousand-Year Door with the settings that the game's table gives it. The game places its effects in the world and can change their volumes and pans as they play. A rip plays each of Rebel Strike's effects at the volume and the pan of its group, without the reverb that a level gives it.
- The code of Tales of Symphonia and Rogue Leader can give an effect a pan. A rip plays each of their effects in the centre. Star Fox Adventures sends its effects to a reverb in some places of its world. Resident Evil Zero sends its effects to an aux bus. A rip plays the effects of both games without those sends.
- 19 of Rebel Strike's effects jump back through GOTO steps of their macros. The ripper doesn't find those loops. Their rips play for ten minutes, then fade.
- Some of Tales of Symphonia's and Rogue Leader's effects keep going until the game stops them. The ripper finds no loop in them. Their rips play for ten minutes, then fade.
- Four of Metroid Prime's effects and one of Metroid Prime 2: Echoes' play only some of the times that the game starts them. In a rip, each one makes a random choice that silences it. The ripper skips it. One more of Metroid Prime 2: Echoes' effects, `SFXspb_a_needlexp_00_oneshot`, tests a variable of MusyX's macros. In a rip, the test skips its sample. The ripper skips the effect.
- Eight of Rogue Leader's songs play a stream along with sequenced notes: The Battle Of Yavin I and III, Hyperspace, Imperial Attack, The Asteroid Field, Celebration & End Titles, and two songs that the music hall doesn't list. A rip of one of them would play only its notes. The ripper leaves them out.
- Some of Resident Evil Zero's cutscene songs start with a rest that times the music to the scene. A rip plays the rest. `bgm_78` is silent for 53 s before its music starts, `bgm_79` for 19 s and `bgm_88` for 16 s.
- The continuous sounds of Animal Crossing and its Japanese releases, such as rivers, the sea and insects, aren't ripped. Neither are the villagers' voices.
- The Bonus Disc's program holds a second set of 825 effects that it never loads. GCSF doesn't rip them.
- Some of The Wind Waker's effects read the reverb of the room that they play in. A few of them make no sound in a rip with any value. The ripper skips them. `docs/gcsf.md` lists them.
- A rip plays `LuigiSings` in 17 of Luigi's Mansion's states. Each one takes the most health of its band. In the game, the tempo falls further as Luigi loses health within a band. While the lights are on, the tempo follows Luigi's steps. A rip of the lit room plays the tempo of Luigi standing still.
- 10 of Super Mario Sunshine's effects loop inside a subroutine. The ripper doesn't find those loops. Their rips play for ten minutes.
- 181 of Donkey Kong Jungle Beat's sound effects loop, but the ripper doesn't find their loops. They play for ten minutes, then fade. A chord effect's keys depend on the stage, the chord of the moment and the keys before. A rip plays each chord effect in one state: over the music of one stage, from the first of the game's records that names it. Some chord effects play no notes in that state. The ripper skips them. Some others repeat their steps until the game stops them. They play for ten minutes, then fade. Later in a combo, the game moves some chord effects on by more steps. A rip plays only the steps that start by themselves. Some effects that aren't chord effects take their notes from values that the game sets as it plays them. They play nothing in a rip. The ripper skips them.
- 16 of Mario Kart: Double Dash!!'s level sounds, the engines' ramps, play for ten minutes, then fade. The ripper doesn't find their loops. Two pieces of music, the star's and the Chain Chomp's, loop every 6 and 11 seconds but play for 4 min 27 s before the fade.
- Some of Pikmin's effects loop inside a subroutine. The ripper doesn't find those loops. Their rips play for ten minutes, then fade. A rip plays the formation sound for one move only: a full move straight ahead. Pikmin's chatter follows the game's state through values that the game sets. The ripper leaves it out.
- Twilight Princess HD lacks one of the Wii's wave archives. `gcsfrip` warns about it. The sounds that use it play without its waves.
- The XMPlay plugin and the 32-bit foobar2000 component run the emulation more slowly than the 64-bit programs. On a slow computer, the busiest pieces may not play in real time in them.
- The output is stereo. Surround isn't played.
